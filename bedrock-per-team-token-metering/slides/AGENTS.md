# Slides: bedrock-per-team-token-metering

Slidev deck for "Who Spent All the Tokens? Per-Team AI Cost Control as Code". 60 minutes, demo-only, presented by Adam Gordon Bell and Engin Diri on 2026-11-04. 25 slides; the per-slide run of show (timings), the pre-bake checklist and the demo run card live in the gitignored `INTERNAL.md`, not in speaker notes.

## Shape
Most of the hour sets up the problem; the demo is short and mostly a tour of code that is already deployed; the close spends more time on why Pulumi than on alternatives. Slides carry pictures and a few words; what to say goes in the speaker notes.

## Story
1. Show, don't tell: housekeeping (two lines: questions in the Q&A tab; code, slides and recording by email) and a five-item agenda, then five problem slides. Three are the screenshot and nothing else (`bleed: true` in the frontmatter hides the accent bar, the title, the footer and the corner strip; `.bleed` is the image at `object-fit: cover`, `.bleed__src` the mono source line overlaid bottom right; what the picture says goes in the notes). Engin's speaker slide comes before Adam's (host last). In order: TechCrunch, Uber caps employee AI spending after blowing through its 2026 budget in four months (Forbes blocks the screenshot tool, so TechCrunch carries the Uber story in reader mode); "It is not an Uber story", a quote slide: Gergely Orosz's photo (cropped from the Pragmatic Engineer page, `public/img/gergely-orosz.png`) beside two verbatim lines from "The Pulse: token spend breaks budgets, what next?" (30 Apr 2026, fifteen companies): "$500 a day on Claude Code" and the $100-per-user limit "breaking when reality hits"; the DN42 story, full-bleed (`public/img/dn42-agent-bill.png`, Lan Tian's blog, 13 May 2026, 1,467 points on HN as item 48500012): a hobbyist's agent provisioned five m8g.12xlarge instances and ran up a $6,531.30 AWS bill in under a week, the operator asked for donations; The Register, Claude Opus on Bedrock, $30,141 for April, Cost Anomaly Detection does not cover Marketplace charges (14 May 2026); The Register, $82K Gemini bill from a leaked key (3 Mar 2026), whose notes carry the "bots on keys have nobody to stop them" line. Stories are about accidental overspend only: a company, a team, a person or a pet project that found out from the bill. Nothing about tokenmaxxing or leaderboards (that is the opposite of the point; the Meta leaderboard, The Next Web, three HN comments and the bots-in-a-loop slide were cut 2026-11-03), no "measure outcomes not tokens" pieces, nothing secondhand (the "$47K loop", "$4,200 in 63 hours" and "$72K retry" figures circulate without a reachable primary source and are not used).
2. Possible solutions, three cards: seat plans (per person, per tool; not for bots or internal apps on a key), spend caps on AWS (Budgets and Cost Anomaly Detection read the bill, a day late, account-wide, and miss Marketplace), and per-team metering with a stop, as code (today).
3. The problem: our org builds on AWS and Bedrock. One account, one bill, one line called Amazon Bedrock. Nobody can say which team spent it, nothing stops a team spending it, the cost-tag answer arrives a day late.
4. Case study: `search` (Slack RAG assistant, Nova 2 Lite, cc-1001), `support` (ticket triage on ECS, Nova 2 Lite, cc-2002), `dev` (Claude Code on Bedrock via `ANTHROPIC_MODEL` + `CLAUDE_CODE_USE_BEDROCK=1`). Goal line: per-team limits and alerts on Bedrock spend, on one account. Which teams the demo deploys is said, not shown.
5. What we'll build: the architecture diagram (three clients, each through its own role and tagged application inference profile into Bedrock; the invocation log feeds per-team metric filters and an alarm). No caption; the notes carry the walk. Then "What is in the repo": the code map (`diagrams/code-map.png`): `Pulumi.dev.yaml` (the teams list) feeds `index.ts`, which maps each team onto `meteredModelAccess.ts` (five resources) and creates `tokenCutoff.ts` with its handler `cutoff/index.mjs`; `policy/rules.ts` joins by a dotted "at preview" edge; `scripts/` sits on the AWS side. It is the opening of the code tour, so beat A starts from a picture instead of a cold editor.
6. The demo: a divider whose notes hold the four beats (the finished thing, one up; call and trip; the list and the preview; the stop). Bottom right of the divider, small: the repo QR code (same URL as the Questions slide, which assumes PR #274 is merged) and two click links for the presenter, the stack in Pulumi Cloud and the CloudWatch alarms console in us-east-1. These are cues, not content; keep them muted. Nothing is deployed before the session beyond setup and the Policy Group. Live, Adam reads `Pulumi.dev.yaml` (two default limits, three teams with per-team overrides: search, support, dev as on the case-study slide) and runs `pulumi up`, 36 resources, touring `index.ts`, `meteredModelAccess.ts`, `cutoff/index.mjs` and `policy/rules.ts` while it builds, then the `teamsOut` output (role, profile, both limits per team) and the stack in Pulumi Cloud; calls as each team, reads the log, and fires `scripts/trip-alarm.sh search`; previews a fourth team (eight resources) and puts the file back; shows the cross-team AccessDenied and breaks and fixes the preview with plain `pulumi` commands (the policy pack is enforced by the organization's `bedrock-metering` Policy Group, so no `--policy-pack` flag; `scripts/policy-enable.sh` sets that up); then `scripts/check-cutoff.sh` shows the alarm in ALARM, the cutoff function's log line, the Deny on the search role, the printed Converse call failing as search and succeeding as support, and `restore.sh` lifts it. TUTORIAL.md is the from-scratch path for people who clone the repo.
7. What we just saw: the three stop lanes as diagrams (the policy pack at preview, IAM at call time, and at the minute mark the alarm calling the cutoff function, which puts a Deny on the team role; sticky, a human lifts it with `scripts/restore.sh`). The clocks from the README's live runs (record ~15 s, metrics ~1 min, alarm 40 to 90 s, Deny the same second, invoice tags up to a day) are in the notes.
8. Go further: one slide of four pointer cards with URLs, no walkthrough. Client OpenTelemetry (AWS guidance for Claude Code on Bedrock), an LLM gateway (LiteLLM), cost allocation tags + AWS Budgets (the `enableBudget` flag, not demoed), Bedrock Mantle Projects. The trade-offs and the caveats (Agents/Knowledge Bases call under a service role; prompt-cache tokens are separate counts; Responses API on bedrock-runtime rejects application profiles; one profile per team and model; the cutoff is token-rate based, sticky and silent, dollars still come from tags and Budgets a day later; Mantle is bedrock-runtime-only territory for invocation logging, Claude Code defaults to bedrock-runtime unless `CLAUDE_CODE_USE_MANTLE` is set) live in that slide's notes.
9. Why Pulumi: a divider, then three slides of three short lines each, no footer line: not a config DSL (real languages, loops and tests, the language coding agents write); a team is one entry in a list (config entry → one component → five resources); ESC: no key to leak (OIDC, short-lived credentials; callback to the $82K key story). Then "And there is so much more", built natively: five cards, Pulumi IaC large in the centre, Neo, ESC, Insights and IDP around it, each with a Phosphor icon and the one-line tagline from pulumi.com (checked 2026-11-03; "Supergraph" and "Workflow" are not product names there). The earlier marketing graphic is kept as `public/img/pulumi-platform.png` / `pulumi-platform-full.png` but is not on a slide.
10. Wrap: Where we will be next (KubeCon NA Salt Lake City Nov 9–12 with Adam's kagent talk and Platform Engineering Day, the Nov 18 workshop, re:Invent booth 1432, Enterprise DevOps Summit Berlin; sources: pulumi.com/events and the Notion workshop schedule, read 2026-11-03), Continue your Pulumi journey (Slack, Pulumi Cloud, next workshops, with icons; the signup card says everything in the repo runs on the free edition; the nuance, basic ESC and a locally run policy pack, is in the notes), Questions (the only QR codes: the two presenters and the repo).

## Navigation

Click a stage to jump. Covered stages are struck through or shown as ▪, upcoming as ◦; state is never colour-only.
- `components/DemoBadge.vue` and the `.cue` style (style.css) are unused: the demo has no per-step slides. Commands and handoff cues live in `INTERNAL.md`.
- `components/QRCode.vue`: QR codes on the Questions slide.

Each story slide carries a mono source line (site, author where known, date), overlaid bottom right.
- Diagrams (architecture, code map, the three stop lanes) live in the repo-root `diagrams/` (PNGs plus the Mermaid sources in `*.json`), so they can be opened from the editor during the demo; `public/img/diagrams` is a symlink to that folder. They are pre-rendered in the hand-drawn Excalidraw style via the mermaid-excalidraw exporter with a local patch that honours `<br/>` and shrinks labels to fit their boxes. Edit the JSON and re-render, then flatten onto white (`magick X.png -background white -alpha remove -alpha off X.png`): the exporter emits transparent PNGs, which read as black-on-black in GitHub dark mode and other dark viewers. Do not hand-edit the PNGs.

## Rules
- Speaker notes are talking points only. No timing budgets, no staging meta; that goes in `INTERNAL.md`.
- Few words on a slide. A slide is a picture, a diagram, or at most three short lines. No caption or "in the demo" footer under a picture or a list; the words are spoken from the notes.
- No code on slides. The code tour happens in the editor; the run card in `INTERNAL.md` lists the commands verbatim. No program code anywhere in the deck.
- No theme layouts except the cover; no Mermaid blocks in slides (diagrams are pre-rendered PNGs, see Navigation). Brand colours only via `var(--p-*)` tokens; the cue orange is the one exception. Adam is red-green colorblind: never signal state by red or green alone.
- The budget (`enableBudget`) is not demoed; it gets one card on "Go further".
- No invented stats. Every number on a story slide comes from the screenshotted source and is verified against it (an earlier "$7 to $72k" loop figure was attributed to the Microsoft talk and is in neither the talk nor its companion article; it is gone). Cost rates, if ever shown, must match `../README.md`.
- Build: `npm ci && npm run build`. Export: `env -u NODE_OPTIONS npx slidev export slides.md --format png` (default output dir). `vite.config.ts` allows `.ts.net` hosts so the deck can be rehearsed from a phone over the tailnet with `--remote`.
